Alerts

Dokkit tells you when something needs you, on the channels you set up: paging for what cannot wait, a quiet push for today, and email for the morning.

How it decides

An issue alerts once, when it opens, and what it raised is resolved when it closes. Its state picks the channel.

People and rules

Until you name people, an alert goes to the addresses set for each channel. Name them at four levels: the default, a rule, a service and a check. The most specific level that names someone decides.

People

Each person can have their own email, phone, iMessage address, ntfy topic, Pushover key and PagerDuty key. Send a test to one person from their row.

Rules

A rule picks the channel and the people for a kind of issue, a state and a machine. The first rule that fits decides. A service's own routing comes first.

Night

Choose when night starts and ends, and a time zone. A service set not to wake you holds its alerts until the night ends.

Limits

The percent of a disk, or of memory for three minutes running, that needs a look. Leave either blank to switch it off.

When Dokkit is down

Dokkit cannot tell anyone that it is down. Give it an outside heartbeat: an https address it calls every minute, at a service that alerts when the calls stop.

  • When Dokkit stops taking reports or cannot reach its database, it tells you on the loudest channel that is set up, unless you choose one.
  • Lesser trouble, such as an archive folder it cannot write to or a certificate close to running out, has its own choice.
